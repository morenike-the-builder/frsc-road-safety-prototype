MODULE 1
Road Safety Fundamentals

What is Road Safety?

Road safety is about protecting yourself and others on the road. It involves understanding traffic rules, recognizing hazards, and making responsible decisions while driving.

Why Road Safety Matters in Nigeria

Nigeria experiences significant road traffic incidents annually:

  • Speeding - 40% of accidents
  • Reckless driving - Unsafe lane changes
  • Driver fatigue - Slower reaction times
  • Poor vehicle maintenance - Brake failure
  • Lack of hazard awareness - Not seeing dangers

Your Responsibility as a Driver

  • Obey traffic laws and signals
  • Maintain your vehicle in safe condition
  • Drive only when alert and sober
  • Respect other road users
  • Anticipate and avoid hazards

MODULE 2
Traffic Signs & Road Markings

The Three Types of Traffic Signs

1. Regulatory Signs (Red & White)

Tell you what you MUST do or what you MUST NOT do.

  • Stop Sign - Come to a complete stop
  • No Entry - Do not drive down this road
  • Speed Limit - Maximum legal speed
  • One-Way - Traffic flows in one direction

2. Warning Signs (Yellow Diamond)

Alert you to potential hazards. Slow down and be prepared.

  • Pedestrian Crossing - Watch for people
  • School Zone - Children may be present
  • Slippery Road - Reduce speed
  • Construction - Road work in progress

Road Markings on Pavement

  • White Broken Line - You CAN cross
  • White Solid Line - You CANNOT cross
  • Yellow Solid - No passing zone

MODULE 3
Road Rules & Regulations

Nigerian Road Traffic Laws

Established by the FRSC to protect all road users.

Speed Limits in Nigeria

  • Urban areas: 50 km/h maximum
  • Highways: 100 km/h maximum
  • School zones: 40 km/h

Lane Discipline

  • Keep right except when overtaking
  • Signal before changing lanes
  • Never drive on shoulder
  • Leave adequate distance between vehicles
  • The 2-second rule: Keep 2 seconds behind car ahead

Mandatory Requirements

  • Seat belts - All passengers must wear
  • Valid license - Must carry at all times
  • Insurance - Third-party insurance mandatory
  • Roadworthiness - Vehicle must pass inspection

MODULE 4
Defensive Driving

What is Defensive Driving?

Anticipating hazards and taking action to avoid accidents. Assume other drivers may make mistakes and be prepared.

The SIPDE Method

  • Scan - Look 200+ meters ahead
  • Identify - Spot potential hazards
  • Predict - Anticipate what might happen
  • Decide - Choose your action
  • Execute - Perform the action smoothly

The 2-Second Rule

  • Pick a fixed object ahead
  • When the car ahead passes it, count: "one-thousand-one, one-thousand-two"
  • You should reach the object after counting
  • If sooner, you're too close
  • In bad weather, use 4-second rule

Common Hazards to Avoid

  • Tailgating - Following too closely
  • Weaving through traffic
  • Driving in blind spots
  • Road rage - Never engage
  • Poor visibility - Reduce speed
