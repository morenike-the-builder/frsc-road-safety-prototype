MODULE 1
Introduction to Road Safety

What is Road Safety?

Road safety is about protecting yourself and others on the road. It involves understanding traffic rules, recognizing hazards, and making responsible decisions while driving.

Why Road Safety Matters in Nigeria

Nigeria experiences significant road traffic incidents annually. The leading causes are:

  • Speeding - Accounts for 40% of accidents
  • Reckless driving - Unsafe lane changes, tailgating
  • Driver fatigue - Tired drivers have slower reaction times
  • Poor vehicle maintenance - Brake failure, tire blowouts
  • Lack of hazard awareness - Not seeing dangers ahead

The Road User Responsibility

As a driver, you have legal and moral responsibilities:

  • Obey traffic laws and signals
  • Maintain your vehicle in safe condition
  • Drive only when alert and sober
  • Respect other road users
  • Anticipate and avoid hazards

  • Traffic signs, markings, and regulations

MODULE 2
Traffic Signs & Road Markings

The Three Types of Traffic Signs

1. Regulatory Signs (Red & White)

These signs tell you what you MUST do or what you MUST NOT do. Violating them is illegal.

  • Stop Sign (Red & White) - You must come to a complete stop
  • No Entry (Red Circle) - Do not drive down this road
  • Speed Limit (White Rectangle) - Maximum legal speed
  • One-Way (White Arrow) - Traffic flows in one direction only

2. Warning Signs (Yellow Diamond)

These alert you to potential hazards ahead. Slow down and be prepared.

  • Pedestrian Crossing - Watch for people crossing
  • School Zone - Children may be present
  • Slippery Road - Reduce speed in wet/sandy conditions
  • Construction Ahead - Road work in progress

3. Informational Signs (Blue/Green)

These provide helpful information about services and directions.

  • Gas Station - Fuel available ahead
  • Hospital - Medical services nearby
  • Directional Signs - Distance and direction to cities

Road Markings on Pavement

  • White Broken Line - You CAN cross (change lanes)
  • White Solid Line - You CANNOT cross
  • Yellow Solid Line - No passing zone
  • Center Markings - Show where center of road is

MODULE 3
Road Rules & Regulations

Nigerian Road Traffic Laws

These rules are established by the FRSC (Federal Road Safety Commission) to protect all road users.

Speed Limits in Nigeria

  • Urban areas: 50 km/h maximum
  • Highways: 100 km/h maximum
  • School zones: 40 km/h during school hours
  • Residential areas: 40 km/h

Lane Discipline

Proper lane use prevents head-on collisions and accidents:

  • Keep right except when overtaking
  • Signal before changing lanes
  • Never drive on shoulder or sidewalk
  • Leave adequate distance between vehicles
  • The 2-second rule: Keep 2 seconds behind the car ahead

Right of Way Rules

  • At intersections without signals, give way to the right
  • Yield to emergency vehicles (ambulance, fire, police)
  • Always give way to pedestrians
  • At roundabouts, cars inside have priority

Mandatory Requirements

  • Seat belts - All passengers must wear
  • Headlights - Use from dusk to dawn
  • Valid license - Must carry at all times
  • Insurance - Third-party insurance mandatory
  • Roadworthiness - Vehicle must pass safety inspection

Prohibited Behaviors

  • Driving under the influence of drugs or alcohol
  • Using mobile phone while driving
  • Driving without valid license
  • Overloading vehicle beyond capacity
  • Driving with faulty brakes or lights

MODULE 4
Defensive Driving

What is Defensive Driving?

Defensive driving means anticipating hazards and taking action to avoid accidents. It's about assuming other drivers may make mistakes and being prepared.

The SIPDE Method

A proven technique used by professional drivers:

  • Scan - Look 200+ meters ahead and check mirrors
  • Identify - Spot potential hazards
  • Predict - Anticipate what might happen
  • Decide - Choose your action
  • Execute - Perform the action smoothly

Common Hazards to Avoid

  • Tailgating - Following too closely reduces reaction time
  • Weaving through traffic - Increases collision risk
  • Driving in blind spots - Make yourself visible
  • Road rage - Never engage with aggressive drivers
  • Poor visibility conditions - Reduce speed in rain/fog

Safe Following Distance

The 2-second rule helps maintain safe distance:

  • Pick a fixed object ahead (pole, sign)
  • When the car ahead passes it, start counting: "one-thousand-one, one-thousand-two"
  • You should reach the object after you finish counting
  • If you reach it sooner, you're too close
  • In bad weather, use 4-second rule instead

Emergency Maneuvers

  • Sudden stop - Keep both hands on wheel, don't swerve
  • Swerving - Only when necessary; check for oncoming traffic
  • Brake failure - Use handbrake, downshift, find safe place to stop
  • Hydroplaning - Don't brake; ease off accelerator and steer straight
